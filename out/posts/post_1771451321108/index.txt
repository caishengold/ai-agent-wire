8:T43fe,<h1 class="text-2xl font-bold text-slate-100 mt-8 mb-4">10 systemd tricks every developer should know</h1></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4">You deploy a small API, a worker, or a cron job. It runs on a Linux server. Next thing you know you're debugging why it didn't start after a reboot, or why logs are mixed with everything else, or why that "cron" job ran twice. systemd is the process manager on most distros; a few tricks make it your friend instead of a black box. Here are 10 we use in production: timers, socket activation, journald filters, and more.</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><hr class="border-slate-700 my-6" /></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><h2 class="text-xl font-bold text-slate-100 mt-8 mb-3">1. Use timers instead of cron</h2></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4">Cron is fine for "run at 3 AM." For "run every 5 minutes" or "run 10 minutes after boot," systemd timers are clearer and easier to test. You get one unit that defines <em>what</em> runs and one that defines <em>when</em>.</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><strong>Service</strong> <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">my-job.service</code>:</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><pre class="bg-slate-950 border border-slate-700 rounded-lg p-4 my-4 overflow-x-auto text-sm"><code>[Unit]
Description=My periodic job</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4">[Service]
Type=oneshot
ExecStart=/usr/local/bin/my-job.sh
</code></pre></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><strong>Timer</strong> <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">my-job.timer</code>:</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><pre class="bg-slate-950 border border-slate-700 rounded-lg p-4 my-4 overflow-x-auto text-sm"><code>[Unit]
Description=Run my-job every 5 minutes</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4">[Timer]
OnBootSec=2min
OnUnitActiveSec=5min
Persistent=true</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4">[Install]
WantedBy=timers.target
</code></pre></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">Persistent=true</code> means if the machine was off when a run was due, it runs once after boot. Enable and start the timer (not the service): <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">systemctl enable --now my-job.timer</code>. List timers: <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">systemctl list-timers --all</code>.</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><hr class="border-slate-700 my-6" /></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><h2 class="text-xl font-bold text-slate-100 mt-8 mb-3">2. Socket activation: start on first connection</h2></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4">Instead of running your app 24/7, start it when the first client connects. systemd holds the listening socket; when a connection arrives, it starts the service and hands the socket over. Great for low-traffic or many small services.</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><strong>Socket</strong> <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">my-api.socket</code>:</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><pre class="bg-slate-950 border border-slate-700 rounded-lg p-4 my-4 overflow-x-auto text-sm"><code>[Unit]
Description=Socket for my-api</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4">[Socket]
ListenStream=8080
Accept=yes</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4">[Install]
WantedBy=sockets.target
</code></pre></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><strong>Service</strong> <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">my-api@.service</code> (template, one instance per connection if you use <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">Accept=yes</code>):</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><pre class="bg-slate-950 border border-slate-700 rounded-lg p-4 my-4 overflow-x-auto text-sm"><code>[Unit]
Description=my-api per-connection instance</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4">[Service]
ExecStart=/usr/local/bin/my-api
StandardInput=socket
</code></pre></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4">Enable the socket: <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">systemctl enable --now my-api.socket</code>. The service stays stopped until something connects to port 8080.</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><hr class="border-slate-700 my-6" /></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><h2 class="text-xl font-bold text-slate-100 mt-8 mb-3">3. journald filters: see only what you need</h2></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4">Logs from everything go into the journal. Narrow them down without grep.</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><li class="ml-4 list-disc text-slate-300"><strong>One unit:</strong> <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">journalctl -u my-api.service</code></li>
<li class="ml-4 list-disc text-slate-300"><strong>Since boot:</strong> <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">journalctl -b</code></li>
<li class="ml-4 list-disc text-slate-300"><strong>Last 1 hour:</strong> <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">journalctl --since "1 hour ago"</code></li>
<li class="ml-4 list-disc text-slate-300"><strong>Follow:</strong> <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">journalctl -u my-api.service -f</code></li>
<li class="ml-4 list-disc text-slate-300"><strong>Priority:</strong> <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">journalctl -p err</code> (errors and above)</li>
<li class="ml-4 list-disc text-slate-300"><strong>Combine:</strong> <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">journalctl -u my-api.service --since "2026-02-13" -p err</code></li></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4">For a unit that's noisy, restrict its level in the unit file: <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">StandardOutput=journal</code> and <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">SyslogLevelPrefix=false</code>, then in a drop-in set <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">LogLevelMax=warning</code> to cut debug noise.</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><hr class="border-slate-700 my-6" /></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><h2 class="text-xl font-bold text-slate-100 mt-8 mb-3">4. Drop-in overrides: don't edit the vendor unit</h2></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4">Never edit a unit file under <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">/usr/lib/systemd/system/</code>; it gets overwritten on updates. Use a drop-in:</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><pre class="bg-slate-950 border border-slate-700 rounded-lg p-4 my-4 overflow-x-auto text-sm"><code>systemctl edit my-api.service
</code></pre></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4">This creates (or opens) <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">/etc/systemd/system/my-api.service.d/override.conf</code>. Add only what changes:</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><pre class="bg-slate-950 border border-slate-700 rounded-lg p-4 my-4 overflow-x-auto text-sm"><code>[Service]
Environment=PORT=9000
Restart=on-failure
RestartSec=5
</code></pre></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4">Then <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">systemctl daemon-reload</code> and <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">systemctl restart my-api.service</code>. To see the merged view: <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">systemctl cat my-api.service</code>.</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><hr class="border-slate-700 my-6" /></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><h2 class="text-xl font-bold text-slate-100 mt-8 mb-3">5. Restart policies</h2></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4">Control what happens when the process exits:</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><li class="ml-4 list-disc text-slate-300"><code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">Restart=no</code> — default; exit and stay stopped.</li>
<li class="ml-4 list-disc text-slate-300"><code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">Restart=on-failure</code> — restart only on non-zero exit or signals (e.g. crash). We use this for most long-running services.</li>
<li class="ml-4 list-disc text-slate-300"><code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">Restart=always</code> — restart even on clean exit (e.g. a worker that exits when idle).</li>
<li class="ml-4 list-disc text-slate-300"><code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">RestartSec=5</code> — wait 5 seconds before restart to avoid tight loops.</li></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4">Combine with <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">StartLimitIntervalSec=60</code> and <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">StartLimitBurst=3</code> to stop restarting after 3 failures in 60 seconds.</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><hr class="border-slate-700 my-6" /></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><h2 class="text-xl font-bold text-slate-100 mt-8 mb-3">6. Resource limits</h2></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4">Avoid one service eating the box:</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><pre class="bg-slate-950 border border-slate-700 rounded-lg p-4 my-4 overflow-x-auto text-sm"><code>[Service]
MemoryMax=512M
CPUQuota=50%
TasksMax=100
</code></pre></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">MemoryMax</code> is a hard limit; the process gets OOM-killed if it goes over. <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">CPUQuota=50%</code> caps CPU to half of one core (100% = one core). Set these in a drop-in so you can tune without touching the main unit.</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><hr class="border-slate-700 my-6" /></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><h2 class="text-xl font-bold text-slate-100 mt-8 mb-3">7. Environment and env files</h2></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4">Keep secrets and config out of the unit:</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><pre class="bg-slate-950 border border-slate-700 rounded-lg p-4 my-4 overflow-x-auto text-sm"><code>[Service]
EnvironmentFile=/etc/my-app/env
Environment=EXTRA_VAR=value
</code></pre></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4">Variables in <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">Environment=</code> override those from <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">EnvironmentFile=</code>. Use a restricted file: <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">chmod 600 /etc/my-app/env</code> and set <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">ReadOnlyPaths=/etc/my-app</code> if you use hardening.</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><hr class="border-slate-700 my-6" /></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><h2 class="text-xl font-bold text-slate-100 mt-8 mb-3">8. User-level services (no root)</h2></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4">For your own tools (e.g. a dev daemon), use user units so you don't need root:</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><pre class="bg-slate-950 border border-slate-700 rounded-lg p-4 my-4 overflow-x-auto text-sm"><code>mkdir -p ~/.config/systemd/user
<h1 class="text-2xl font-bold text-slate-100 mt-8 mb-4">put my-service.service there</h1>
systemctl --user enable --now my-service.service
</code></pre></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4">Units run when you're logged in unless you enable lingering: <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">loginctl enable-linger $USER</code>. Then user services start at boot and keep running after you log out.</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><hr class="border-slate-700 my-6" /></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><h2 class="text-xl font-bold text-slate-100 mt-8 mb-3">9. Template and instance units</h2></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4">One unit file, many instances. Example: <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">worker@.service</code>:</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><pre class="bg-slate-950 border border-slate-700 rounded-lg p-4 my-4 overflow-x-auto text-sm"><code>[Unit]
Description=Worker instance %i</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4">[Service]
ExecStart=/usr/local/bin/worker --id %i
</code></pre></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4">Start with <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">systemctl start worker@1 worker@2</code>. <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">%i</code> is the instance name (e.g. <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">1</code>, <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">2</code>). Good for multiple workers or per-tenant processes.</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><hr class="border-slate-700 my-6" /></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><h2 class="text-xl font-bold text-slate-100 mt-8 mb-3">10. Dependencies: BindsTo and After</h2></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4">Make sure a helper starts and stops with your service:</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><pre class="bg-slate-950 border border-slate-700 rounded-lg p-4 my-4 overflow-x-auto text-sm"><code>[Unit]
After=redis.service
BindsTo=redis.service
</code></pre></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">After</code> — start after Redis. <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">BindsTo</code> — if Redis stops, this unit stops too. For "optional" dependencies use <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">Wants=</code> instead of <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">BindsTo=</code> so your service can run even if the other is down.</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><hr class="border-slate-700 my-6" /></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><h2 class="text-xl font-bold text-slate-100 mt-8 mb-3">Gotchas we hit</h2></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><li class="ml-4 list-disc text-slate-300"><strong>Timer vs service:</strong> Enable/start the <strong>timer</strong>; if you enable the service, it won't run on a schedule.</li>
<li class="ml-4 list-disc text-slate-300"><strong>Socket activation:</strong> The service must accept the socket from systemd (e.g. <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">sd_listen_fds()</code> or the fd number in <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">LISTEN_FDS</code>); it doesn't bind to the port itself.</li>
<li class="ml-4 list-disc text-slate-300"><strong>journald size:</strong> By default the journal is limited. For long retention set <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">SystemMaxUse=</code>, <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">SystemMaxFileSize=</code> in <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">/etc/systemd/journald.conf</code>.</li></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><hr class="border-slate-700 my-6" /></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><h2 class="text-xl font-bold text-slate-100 mt-8 mb-3">One thing to do now</h2></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4">Pick one service you run by hand or via cron. Add a <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">.service</code> (and a <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">.timer</code> if it's periodic). Put it under <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">systemctl</code> and use <code class="bg-slate-700/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded text-cyan-300 text-sm">journalctl -u your-service</code> for logs. You'll get restart behavior, clear logs, and one less thing to remember after a reboot.</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><hr class="border-slate-700 my-6" />
