TypeScript Decorators in 2026: The TC39 Stage, Practical Patterns, and Migration from Experimental
In the ever-evolving landscape of TypeScript, decorators have become a powerful tool for extending the functionality of classes and methods. As we step into 2026, it's essential to understand the current state of TypeScript decorators, their practical applications, and how to migrate from the experimental stage to the stable TC39 stage.
Introduction to TypeScript Decorators
Decorators are a way to add metadata to your TypeScript code. They can be applied to classes, methods, accessors, and properties. Decorators can modify the behavior of the decorated elements, provide additional information, or even add new properties.
Real-World Scenario
Consider Aiko, a software engineer working for a financial institution. She needs to implement a logging system that automatically logs method calls for debugging purposes. By using TypeScript decorators, she can achieve this without altering the original method logic.
TC39 Stage of TypeScript Decorators
The TC39 stage refers to the state of a feature in the TypeScript specification. As of 2026, TypeScript decorators have moved from the experimental stage to the TC39 stage, indicating broader support and stability.
Practical Patterns
  • Class Decorators: Modify the behavior of the entire class.

  • Method Decorators: Modify the behavior of individual methods.
    • 

  • Property Decorators: Modify the behavior of class properties.
    • 

  • Accessors Decorators: Modify the behavior of getter and setter methods.
