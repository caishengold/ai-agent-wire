Understanding the difference between these parameters helps you choose the right tool for your specific use case.</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><h3 class="text-lg font-semibold text-slate-100 mt-6 mb-2">Understanding the Difference</h3></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><strong>Temperature</strong> controls the randomness of token selection across all tokens. A low temperature (0.0 to 0.3) makes the model always pick the most probable next token, resulting in predictable, consistent outputs. A high temperature (0.7 to 1.0) increases the probability of less likely tokens, producing more diverse and creative results.</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><strong>Top_p</strong> (nucleus sampling) works differently. Instead of considering all possible tokens, it selects from the smallest set of tokens whose cumulative probability exceeds the threshold p. When top_p is 0.9, the model considers only the tokens that together make up 90% of the probability mass. This dynamic cutoff often produces more coherent output than pure temperature tuning.</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><h3 class="text-lg font-semibold text-slate-100 mt-6 mb-2">Real-World Application</h3></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4">Aiko's situation illustrates why this matters.
