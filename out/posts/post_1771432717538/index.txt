Their configured fallback model initially failed on their specific document formats. Regular testing of fallback paths revealed the incompatibility before it caused a production incident.</li></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><li class="ml-4 list-disc text-slate-300">Alert fatigue is real. They started with alerts for every failure and quickly learned to distinguish recoverable failures (which get retried automatically) from permanent failures (which need human attention). The alerting system only triggers after retries are exhausted.</li></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><h2 class="text-xl font-bold text-slate-100 mt-8 mb-3">Actionable Takeaways</h2></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><li class="ml-4 list-decimal text-slate-300"><strong>Separate monitoring layers</strong> — Implement both internal heartbeat (for OpenClaw coordination) and external heartbeat (for your observability stack). This provides clear separation between agent health and system health.</li></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><li class="ml-4 list-decimal text-slate-300"><strong>Configure exponential backoff with jitter</strong> — Set initial delays around 5-10 seconds with a multiplier of 1.5-2 and maximum attempts of 3-5 for most external service calls. Untested fallbacks often fail when you need them most.</li></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4"><li class="ml-4 list-decimal text-slate-300"><strong>Alert only after retry exhaustion</strong> — Configure your alerting to notify after all automatic retries fail. This reduces alert noise while ensuring persistent problems reach your team.</li></p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4">Running AI agents unattended requires the same reliability engineering as any critical infrastructure. With proper heartbeat, scheduling, and error recovery, Raj's document processing system now handles over 150,000 documents monthly with less than 0.1% requiring manual intervention. The agent operates continuously across time zones, processing work as it arrives and flagging exceptions for human review during business hours.</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4">The journey from a fragile daemon process to a resilient unattended system took approximately three months of iteration. The initial version handled happy path scenarios well but crumbled under real-world failure conditions. By systematically addressing each failure mode—network blips, memory leaks, external service timeouts, and human errors—Raj built confidence in the system's ability to operate independently.</p><p class="text-slate-300 leading-relaxed mb-4">What makes this approach sustainable is that the monitoring and recovery mechanisms run automatically. The team receives alerts only when human intervention is genuinely needed, freeing them to focus on improving the agent's capabilities rather than babysitting its operation. This is the fundamental promise of unattended AI systems: your agents work while you sleep.
